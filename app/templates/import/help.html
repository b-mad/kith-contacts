Importing contacts

Bring people in from Google Contacts, Outlook, LinkedIn or a spreadsheet in three steps:

  1. Export your contacts from the other app (see below for Google, Outlook and LinkedIn).
  2. Upload the file on the Import page and check the preview.
  3. Press Import. Nothing is saved before that.

Reading the column mapping

A CSV file has a header row with a name for each column. The mapping shows one line per column, and reads from left to right:

Organization Name →goes into Company

  • Left: the column name exactly as it appears in your file.
  • Right: the Contact Manager field that column goes into. Columns the app recognises are chosen for you; the rest say Ignore and are left out.
  • To change one, pick another field from its list and press Update preview. The table underneath shows the first rows as they will be saved.
  • Give each field one column. If two columns go into the same field, the one later in the file wins.
  • A column you don't need — a fax number, a photo link — can stay on Ignore.

Rows marked Error are skipped (usually a row with no name, company or email). Rows marked Duplicate? share an email address, or a name and company, with someone already here or earlier in the file; they are skipped unless you tick Import possible duplicates too.

Tip: fill in Also add everyone to a list — for example “Imported from Google” — so you can find and review the whole batch afterwards.

From Google Contacts

  1. Open Google Contacts in a browser and choose Export.
  2. Pick all contacts, or one label to start small.
  3. Choose Google CSV and export. Upload the downloaded contacts.csv here.

Good to know:

  • Google labels become tags. Google’s built-in “myContacts” and “starred” are left out.
  • Google’s label on each email, phone and address (Home, Mobile, Work…) comes along. The email whose label starts with * becomes the primary one.
  • Numbers labelled as a fax (Work Fax, Home Fax) are left out.
  • When a person has two values in one cell, Google separates them with ::: — for example two mobile numbers, or two addresses spread over the address columns. Each becomes its own entry.
  • Addresses keep their street, city, state, ZIP and country. States are stored as their code (“Kansas” and “Kan” become KS), countries get their two-letter code, and an address with a US state but no country is taken to be in your home country.
  • A row with a company but no person’s name — a doctor’s office, a restaurant — uses the company as its name.
  • If you kept context in a name field — say, a child’s name in Name Suffix — map that column to an unused field such as Works on so it stays searchable.
  • Photos aren’t imported: Google’s photo links point to Google’s servers, and this app never contacts outside services. Add a photo on the card.

From Outlook

In Outlook on the web open People, then Manage contacts › Export contacts, and choose CSV. In the Outlook desktop app use File › Open & Export › Import/Export › Export to a file. Outlook’s column names (First Name, E-mail Address, Business Phone, Company, Job Title, Manager’s Name, Categories…) are recognised. Its business address becomes a work address and its home address a home address.

From LinkedIn

In LinkedIn open Settings › Data privacy › Get a copy of your data and ask for Connections. Upload Connections.csv as it is; the note at the top of the file is skipped, and the profile link goes into LinkedIn profile.

vCard and JSON files

A vCard (.vcf) file from Apple Contacts, Outlook or Google, or a JSON file from “Copy to another instance”, needs no mapping: every field goes to its place, addresses included.

The fields

Column names are matched without regard to capitals, spaces or punctuation, so “E-mail 1 - Value” matches email1value.
